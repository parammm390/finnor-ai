Y2’s computational boundary

When FINNOR changes a policy, information rule or proposed economic arrangement, Y2 computes:
- How independently acting parties may respond.
- How their responses affect other parties.
- Which joint outcomes remain supported.
- Which predictions depend on unresolved preferences, selection assumptions or transport conditions.
- How those supported responses change the permissible business decision.

The model must condition on the changed rule itself. Reusing historical payoff worlds as though counterparties do not adapt is insufficient.

R3 assumes a declared preference/type model. R5’s first profile excludes strategic spillovers between coalitions. X4 identifies digital mechanisms. Y2 must establish supported coupled, policy-dependent strategic response beyond whatever the actual equipped baseline already supplies.

A fixed-world simulator, plausible counterparty roleplay or collection of independent marginal forecasts does not establish this boundary.

The delivered FINNOR runtime must perform the learning and strategic computation. Build agents may implement generic machinery, but cannot supply hidden actor responses or rescue a scored episode and call that autonomous runtime success.

Required computation

1. Resolve the authorized policy/arrangement, economic meanings, participant scope, evidence, rights, current commitments and knowledge cut.
2. Represent actor-local observations, permitted actions/messages and objectives/preferences as explicit hypotheses. Distinguish observed state from latent/private state.
3. Learn policy-conditioned response functions from admitted observations and interventions.
4. Construct a coupled state-transition and observation model, retaining cross-actor dependence and shared regimes.
5. Establish the S3 basis for any causal-response claim. Preserve observationally indistinguishable models when they imply different policy responses.
6. Compute joint responses under an explicit behavioral/equilibrium basis, retaining alternative models, selections and solver gaps.
7. Evaluate withheld policies, spillovers, actor adaptation and regime changes. Calibrate uncertainty and apply the original decision-loss rules.
8. Publish scoped predictions through existing owners, with actual currentness, admission, transport and unsupported-behavior conditions.
9. Demonstrate actual S4 programme evaluation using those supported joint responses, without converting predictions into observed commitments or settlement.
